# Positioning and modular offer

- Status: proposed for owner review
- Working identity: StudioB22
- Product boundary: public commercial website

## Positioning

The studio helps small local organisations turn an unclear or outdated online presence into a
professional website that is understandable, credible, maintainable, and suited to their real
activity. The relationship is deliberately consultative: listen first, define the useful scope,
then deliver through visible milestones without commercial pressure.

### Primary audience

The homepage addresses owners and decision-makers of small organisations in Grasse, the
Alpes-Maritimes, and the Côte d'Azur who need a first professional website or a substantial
redesign and value a guided, methodical relationship.

The first proof set prioritises three broad groups:

1. crafts and local trades;
2. local retail, food, hospitality, and personal services;
3. independent service businesses.

Regulated professions remain a secondary audience. Their projects require an additional review of
sector-specific communication, claims, confidentiality, accessibility, and data constraints before
the scope is accepted.

The studio may accept work outside these groups. The primary audience guides the message; it is not
an exclusion rule.

### Primary and secondary actions

- **Primary action**: request a calm first exchange through one monitored contact channel.
- **Secondary action**: explore truthfully labelled creations relevant to the visitor's sector.
- **Later action**: prepare a non-binding project orientation through the guided diagnostic.

### Communication principles

- Professional, attentive, direct, and reassuring.
- Explain outcomes and responsibilities in business language.
- Show the method instead of claiming to be methodical.
- Explain the benefit of AI and automation without making a vendor or novelty the product.
- Never use artificial urgency, countdowns, unsolicited follow-up, or pressure tactics.
- Never present a concept, mock-up, or demonstrator as paid client work.

## Proposed commercial structure

This structure explains the offer; it is not a contract or a public price list. Every engagement
still receives a written scope, assumptions, exclusions, milestones, and payment schedule.

### Core website foundation

The core foundation is the smallest complete professional website service. It includes:

- a discovery and scoping session;
- one agreed objective, audience, and primary visitor action;
- a concise page map and content responsibilities;
- a project-specific visual direction based on available identity material;
- responsive design and implementation of the agreed public pages;
- integration of client-approved text and media supplied in usable form;
- technical SEO foundations: semantic pages, metadata, sitemap, robots directives, canonical URLs,
  and internal links;
- baseline accessibility, performance, security, and privacy review within the agreed feature set;
- preview, structured feedback, acceptance, and production deployment preparation;
- handover notes covering ownership, routine updates, and known limits;
- transparent use of suitable AI, automation, analysis, testing, and documentation tools under human
  review and responsibility.

The exact number and type of pages are defined in the written proposal. The word `core` does not
mean unlimited pages, copywriting, branding, integrations, or ongoing support.

### Optional building blocks

| Block | Typical need | Possible deliverables |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Content | The client cannot supply web-ready content | Content workshop, page outlines, rewriting, structured content integration |
| Identity | The current identity is absent or inconsistent | Lightweight visual direction, palette, typography, usage guidance; full branding only by separate scope |
| Visual assets | The site lacks suitable imagery or graphics | Art direction, asset selection, image preparation, illustration or photography coordination |
| Conversion | Visitors need a more guided next step | Enquiry form, booking link, project diagnostic, newsletter or other approved interaction |
| Visibility | Search demand justifies additional work | Search research, local SEO content, structured data review, measurement plan, post-launch improvements |
| Commerce | The business needs online transactions | Focused feasibility and scope for catalogue, payment, fulfilment, legal, and operational constraints |
| Support | The client wants continuing help | Maintenance, content updates, monitoring, reporting, improvement sessions, or training |
| Integration | The website must connect to another service | Provider review, data flow, implementation, error handling, documentation, and operational test |

Each block may be added later when the client's needs evolve. A block is not enabled merely because
the technology makes it possible.

### Client responsibilities

The client remains responsible for:

- factual accuracy and approval of supplied content;
- permission to use names, trademarks, images, testimonials, and other assets;
- timely decisions and consolidated feedback from authorised stakeholders;
- legal or regulated-profession review that requires qualified advice;
- provider subscriptions, domain ownership, and third-party fees unless the proposal states otherwise.

### Not included unless explicitly quoted

- unlimited revisions or unspecified pages;
- full brand strategy, professional photography, translation, or complete copywriting;
- e-commerce, membership, authentication, client portals, payment, or document workflows;
- legal advice or certification of legal, regulatory, accessibility, or security compliance;
- recurring content entry, maintenance, SEO campaigns, analytics reporting, or provider fees;
- migration or publication of every experimental repository.

## Delivery method

The public method presents three phases and seven decision milestones. The milestones are control
points, not promises of identical duration for every engagement. Each proposal maps its deliverables,
dates, acceptance criteria, and staged payments to this method.

### Phase 1 — Understand and commit

| Milestone | Purpose | Decision and client action | Commercial gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1. Project orientation | Understand the activity, audience, current situation, useful outcome, constraints, and investment orientation. | The client supplies available context and identifies the people authorised to decide. Both parties decide whether a structured proposal is appropriate. | No project commitment is implied by the first exchange. Any paid discovery work is identified before it begins. |
| 2. Scope approval | Turn the need into a bounded objective, page and feature scope, responsibilities, assumptions, exclusions, schedule, and acceptance path. | The client reviews one written proposal, resolves open assumptions, and approves the scope and contractual documents. | The initial contractual payment activates the project and reserves delivery capacity. No production work starts before this gate. |

### Phase 2 — Design and deliver

| Milestone | Purpose | Decision and client action | Commercial gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 3. Content and direction readiness | Establish the content structure, available assets, responsibilities, and visual direction before detailed production. | The client supplies or approves the required content and rights, then validates one consolidated direction. | Missing client inputs may move the schedule. Additional content or identity work requires an approved scope change. |
| 4. Experience approval | Validate hierarchy, key journeys, responsive intent, and representative screens before full implementation. | The client provides consolidated feedback through the agreed channel and approves the experience baseline. | Approval unlocks full implementation. Reopening an approved direction is handled as a documented change. |
| 5. Build verification | Implement the approved scope and verify content, responsive behaviour, accessibility, performance, security, privacy, and search foundations. | The client reviews the complete preview against the agreed acceptance criteria and reports factual corrections or scoped defects. | The next staged payment becomes due at the contractual verification gate, before production release preparation. |

### Phase 3 — Launch and evolve

| Milestone | Purpose | Decision and client action | Commercial gate |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6. Launch and handover | Complete final checks, publish through the approved infrastructure, and transfer the agreed documentation, ownership information, and operating guidance. | The client gives written launch approval, confirms legal and factual content, and receives the agreed handover material. | The launch or handover payment defined by the contract is required before the corresponding assets or production release are transferred. |
| 7. Follow-up decision | Review the delivered result, outstanding observations, maintenance needs, and evidence for a later improvement stage. | The client accepts the completed scope, identifies any warranty defect, and decides whether support or a new optional block is useful. | Ongoing support and later improvements are separate, explicit services; there is no automatic renewal or pressure to continue. |

At every milestone, acceptance concerns the written scope and criteria rather than subjective unlimited
revision. A requested change is first clarified for impact on cost, timing, data, and quality; it is
implemented only after written approval when it changes the agreed scope. Payment percentages, due dates,
late-payment terms, intellectual-property transfer, warranty, and termination rules belong to the signed
proposal and contract, not to universal website copy.

## Pricing position

The public site does not publish invented or unsustainable figures. A future `from` price may be
introduced only after the core scope, capacity, direct costs, taxes, risk allowance, support burden,
and target margin have been calculated from real delivery experience.

The discovery form may ask for an investment orientation to assess scope compatibility. The wording
must explain that this is not a pressure tactic or an automatic quote: it helps propose a realistic
path, including a smaller first stage when appropriate.

## Content validation still required

Before final public copy is written, validate the language with a small set of representative local
business owners. Record the words they use for their current problem, their main objections, and what
makes a provider feel trustworthy. Do not fabricate voice-of-customer language in the meantime.

benefits and safeguards rather than list transient vendor names.
